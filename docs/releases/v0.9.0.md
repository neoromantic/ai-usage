## Devices are named user@device

The report names each device by the OS user its collector runs as, at the device's name: `ann@mira-mbp`, `root@build-01`, `hermes@scout`. A person and their laptop read as one consumer, and each user or bot on a shared server reads as one of its own. The name is everywhere a device shows: the header, ATTENTION, SUBSCRIPTIONS' busiest device, DEVICES in both views, the interactive view, `ai-usage status`, the first-run guide, every reason and note ("reinstalled as" included), and the menu bar app ("This Mac: ann@mira-mbp").

- Devices on older releases are named the same way, since every release already sends its OS user.
- The device part is the device's name as before: the host name, or one set with `ai-usage name set` or `AI_USAGE_NAME`. A name that already has an `@` stays as it is, and a device whose user is not known goes by its name alone.
- A Windows device is named by its user without the domain: `ann@DESKTOP-7Q`, not `CORP\ann@DESKTOP-7Q`.
- `ai-usage name` says what the team sees, as `Build bot (the team sees ci@Build bot)`, and `ai-usage team` lists devices the same way.
- The status view of DEVICES, and the menu bar app's Team window on Status, have no USER column any more: the name says it.
- A name too wide for its column is cut in its device part first, as `kim@Mac.localdoma…`.

## Why a subscription has no reading

When a Claude, Codex, or Grok subscription has no reading, the report says why. SUBSCRIPTIONS shows what to do, such as `update codex`, in place of its bar, and ATTENTION has the whole reason. It comes from a device the account is logged in to, this one first, then a teammate's, so the team view tells you what is wrong on someone else's machine:

```
 NO READING  codex leo@studio.dev    this codex cannot read its limits on leo@leo-air: update codex
```

- Codex: too old to read its limits (`update codex`), a login it refused (`run codex login`), timed out, could not start (`reinstall codex`), or exited on its config (`run codex app-server to see why`).
- Grok: a log with no credit usage yet, or no signed-in user (`run grok and sign in`).
- Claude Code: `/usage` timed out, failed, or could not read the usage, as well as the update notes it had before.
- On a machine with several folders for one tool, the reason names the folder, and only the account logged in there gets it.
- ATTENTION lists it as NO READING while the subscription was used in the last 7 days, and the menu bar app shows it under "Not read".
- A tool's note that is no failure, such as a Grok log with no credit usage yet, is dim in `ai-usage status` and in the app's device details, not red.

The reasons are in the report's own words: they leave out what the tool printed, such as an address, and are at most 200 bytes.

## Drill down in the interactive view

`Enter` picks a row on the page: an ATTENTION line about one account or device, an account in SUBSCRIPTIONS, UTILIZATION, or USAGE, or a machine in DEVICES, a bot's container included. `↑` `↓` (`j` `k`, `g` `G`) move the pick, and `Enter` again, or a click on the picked row, opens its detail in the page's place. `Esc` or `Backspace` goes back to the page as it was, the row still picked, and `Esc` again stops picking. The period keys redraw a detail.

- **An account's detail** has its ATTENTION lines; its plan, the reading and the device it came from, last use, and linked usage; each window with its bar, what is left, its reset, the forecast, the pace, and when it runs out at that pace; how full each window got before its last resets; every machine that spent it, in its current window and over each period, with its share; and this machine's projects on it.
- **A machine's detail** has its ATTENTION lines; its release, last report, last good run, the tools it reads, and each error in full; every account it spent, with its share of each; its share of the team's tokens; and, for this machine, its projects.
- The detail is built from the report alone, so it works on `ai-usage report --from` a saved report too.
- The key bar shows `↵ pick` on the page, `↑↓ pick · ↵ open · esc back` while picking, and `esc back` in a detail. At 80 columns it leaves out `r refresh` to keep `↵ pick`; `r` still works, and `?` lists every key.

## JSON: device names

A reader of `ai-usage --json` should know this:

- New `collector.name` and `team.devices[].name`: what the report calls the device, as `ann@mira-mbp`. `label` and `os_user` keep the parts, unchanged.
- Every string that names a device now says its `name` where it said its label or `host (user)`: `attention[].devices`, `subject`, and `text`, an account's `busiest`, `held_back_by`, `cycles_held_back_by`, and `devices`, `per_device[].device`, `quota.device`, `linked_usage[].devices`, a matrix row's `device`, `no_reading` and the other reasons, `replaced_by`, and a `duplicate` entry's `message`. Match devices by their `device` id, as before; a script that matched a label against these strings needs the name now.
- `no_reading` on a team account now also says why for Codex and Grok, and for a tool that failed. The Grok provider's `error` can say its log has no credit usage yet while its `status` is `ok`.
- The schema version stays 4. `ai-usage report --from` fills `name` in for a report saved by an earlier release, whose other strings keep the names they were saved with. See [docs/json-schema.md](https://github.com/neoromantic/ai-usage/blob/v0.9.0/docs/json-schema.md).

## Compatibility and upgrade

**What to do.** Nothing, for a script install: releases from v0.2.5 on install v0.9.0 within 15 minutes, and on a Mac the menu bar app updates with the binary. A Homebrew install updates with `brew upgrade neoromantic/tap/ai-usage` or `ai-usage update`.

**The team.** The snapshot and the relay do not change. Teammates on older releases show with their new names and their no-reading reasons in your report, since both come from what every release already sends.

## Known limitations

- A teammate's device with several folders for one tool, and more than one account logged in to them, gives no reason for a folder's failure: only that device knows which account a folder holds.
- The detail of another machine has no PROJECTS: the report holds only this machine's projects.
- A report saved by an earlier release keeps its old names in ATTENTION and the reasons; only the devices' `name` is filled in.
